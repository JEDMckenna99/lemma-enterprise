The wallet works like a physical driver's license: issuers sign once, sites verify locally, and users carry the credential. Sites can't correlate you with each other — each sees only a site-private PPID, never your name, document, or other PII. The issuer can recognize the same person across re-verification, and that's exactly what makes a ban enforceable.

What lemma.id can and can't see. lemma.id holds the verified-person root that links your credentials for re-verification and revocation — but it does not store your raw ID document and, because checks run locally in the browser, it does not see which sites you visit or when you verify. Relying sites receive only a human verdict and a PPID scoped to their own domain.

What lemma.id does and doesn't do. It raises the cost of a fresh identity from roughly zero to the cost of passing IDV again. That breaks the economics of high-volume, low-value abuse. It is not designed to stop a funded attacker willing to buy a verified identity for a single high-value target — it's designed to make everyday abuse stop paying.
