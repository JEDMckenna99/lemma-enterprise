How billing works

You pay once when a user first binds a credential to your site, and identity verification is included in that price when you require it. Users who already verified on another lemma.id-enabled site skip the ID ceremony at binding. After that, a small monthly fee applies only in months where the user actually comes back and renews. Day-to-day proof checks run on your own servers and are never metered, so there's no per-login line item to worry about.

Billing rules

  1. Proof binding ($0.50, verification included): one-time fee for the user's first credential on your site, with the lemma.id secured by a passkey on first use. If your policy requires a human proof, the identity check is included in the fifty cents. There is no separate IDV charge and no markup hiding in it.
  2. MAU ($0.03): billed for each calendar month a user renews after the month they bound. The binding month itself is never billed as an MAU.
  3. What counts as active: a user whose credential is re-issued that month, which normally happens when the old one expires after about 30 days and they complete the renewal popup. Daily local checks never count toward this.
  4. Same lemma.id, same PPID: renewals issue a fresh credential with the same site-private PPID, so routine continuity never triggers repeat verification. If a user steps up to a human proof later, the PPID stays stable through that too.
  5. Doubt re-entry ($0.33): billed only when a doubted user passes fresh IDV after a site-doubt challenge. If they fail or abandon, you pay nothing for that attempt.
  6. Local checks: unlimited presentations and verifications on your own infrastructure between renewals, never metered.
  7. Per site: each relying site is billed for its own users. PPIDs are pairwise, so there's nothing to correlate between sites.

Example: 1,000 new users in January, 600 return in February

January: 1,000 × $0.50 = $500, verification included. No MAU charges yet, since the binding month is excluded.

February: 600 returning users × $0.03 = $18.00, plus $0.50 for each brand-new user who joins that month.

Worth noting: a user who logs in every single day still counts as one MAU in a month, because billing follows renewals, not logins.

What you're actually paying for

A raw identity check answers one question once, then leaves you holding the user's data. A login provider tells you which account signed in, but not whether it's the same abuser back for round two. The fifty cents buys the thing neither of them gives you: a durable, private handle on the person behind the account, with none of their identity data on your servers.
